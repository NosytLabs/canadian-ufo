Prince George

1 January 1969 BC Multi-witness 53.92, -122.75 view on map

Three unrelated witnesses in British Columbia reported a round object radiating a yellow-orange light, apparently climbing from 2,000 to 10,000 feet in the late afternoon.

The record

Three independent witnesses with no apparent connection to each other is the strongest part of this report. Prince George sits at the confluence of the Nechako and Fraser rivers in central British Columbia, roughly equidistant from Vancouver and the Yukon border; at the time it was the largest city in the BC Interior and a regional air traffic hub served by scheduled carriers and the RCAF's No. 2 Air Navigation School at nearby Dunmore. A round object climbing from 2,000 to 10,000 feet in a short window is a rate and profile inconsistent with known balloon behaviour.

It is included to keep the west in the record: almost every well-documented Canadian case is eastern, and that skew is a property of the archive rather than of the sky. The NRC collection holds more Ontario and Quebec reports partly because those provinces had denser population and more institutional reporting infrastructure; a BC case with multiple witnesses and an apparent altitude change earns inclusion even with thin primary documentation.

Sourcing is thin and no original NRC or DND file has been located for this event. 1969 is the final year of the US Air Force's Project Blue Book, which closed December 1969; a BC sighting with named civilian witnesses could also appear in NRC's correspondence files for that year. UFO*BC maintains a separate history index for British Columbia material and is the most likely repository for the original press accounts.

Sourcing quality

Thin sourcing — verify before citing

This case is included because gaps are worse than thin records, but the sourcing is partial. Do not build on it without reading the original file.
