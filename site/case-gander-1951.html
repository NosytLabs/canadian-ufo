Gander

10 February 1951 NL Airborne 48.95, -54.60 view on map

A United States Navy aircraft bound for Iceland reported a near-collision with a large orange circular object that, in the pilot's words, almost literally flew circles around it.

The record

It is the earliest dated case in this index and it sits in the same place as the 1974 Gander encounters: the Gander terminal was, by 1951, the world's busiest transatlantic refuelling stop. Almost every piston-era flight between North America and Europe staged through Gander; the Royal Canadian Air Force maintained a base there from 1938 and the United States Air Force and Navy operated at both Gander and at Argentia on the Avalon Peninsula. The volume of military and commercial traffic made Gander uniquely likely both to generate airborne reports and to have radar infrastructure close enough to corroborate them.

The report came from a US Navy crew, which means it would have entered American systems as well as Canadian ones. In early 1951 the US Air Force ran Project Grudge, the predecessor of Blue Book; a report of this kind from a Navy crew over Canadian airspace could land in Grudge files, in Navy Bureau of Aeronautics channels, or in both. The Canadian side of the record is a reference rather than a file anyone here has read directly.

Sourcing is a published summary of a Blue Book-era report rather than the report itself. Treat the wording as indicative until the original is found. Project Blue Book records were digitised and are available through the National Archives at College Park; the specific Grudge-era file for a February 1951 Navy sighting over Newfoundland would be the place to start. An ATI request to Library and Archives Canada for Newfoundland DND aviation files from 1951 is the parallel Canadian step.
