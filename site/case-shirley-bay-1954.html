Shirley's Bay

8 August 1954 ON Instrument record 45.37, -75.90 view on map

Wilbert B. Smith's Project Magnet magnetometer at the Shirley's Bay observatory recorded a sharp anomalous dip in Earth's magnetic field. Smith and two colleagues drove immediately to the site; a 1,000-foot overcast made visual confirmation impossible. The Department of Transport terminated the project within days.

The record

Project Magnet was authorised in 1950 and run part-time by Department of Transport engineer Wilbert B. Smith from a small observatory at Shirley's Bay, west of Ottawa. By August 1954 the station was measuring gamma-ray flux, ionospheric signals and magnetometer readings. Smith's core hypothesis was that UFOs reduced Earth's magnetic field locally as a byproduct of propulsion — which is why a sharp dip was the event his instruments were designed to detect.

On 8 August 1954 the magnetometer registered an anomalous dip of approximately 1,000 gamma — well outside the routine daily variation. Smith and two colleagues drove immediately to the observation point, arriving to find dense overcast with a ceiling of about 1,000 feet. Nothing was visible. The instrument record was real; the cause was never established.

The Department of Transport formally terminated Project Magnet in the same month, citing a lack of concrete results. Smith's instrument logs, correspondence with American counterparts at the Naval Electronics Laboratory and the full dataset are held in the Library and Archives Canada collection and are among the most detailed primary documents in the federal UAP file. The LAC query for Ontario DND records returns Smith-era files.
