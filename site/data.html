03

The federal government's own list of what is missing

The Office of the Chief Science Advisor proposed these fourteen actions "for consideration by the Government of Canada" in order to improve coordination and analysis of UAP reports in Canada, promote public trust and mitigate disinformation, enhance scientific rigour and science literacy, and position Canada for greater collaboration with other countries. The report groups them under four headings and does not number them continuously, so neither do we.

Reporting and data oversight

  • Identify a lead. A federal department or agency responsible for managing public UAP data, which "should be a trusted and recognized scientific organization" with an existing international scientific network. The report names the Canadian Space Agency as a candidate.
  • Establish a dedicated service to collect testimonies, investigate cases and post its analyses publicly, convening government, academic and expert partners to follow up with witnesses. The report cites GEIPAN in France as the model.
  • Enhance reporting capacity in civil aviation. Transport Canada should encourage pilots, cabin crews and air traffic controllers to report without fear of stigmatization, and with NAV CANADA analyse the reports to track trends and give pilots explanations.

Communications

  • Support public dialogue through a proactive transparency strategy, bringing together science centres, museums and public libraries.
  • Promote intra-governmental collaboration through an internal directive so the lead body can explain observations to witnesses and correlate new reports with old ones — with departments such as Transport Canada and the RCMP forwarding the data they hold.
  • Improve media relations, responding to inquiries and documenting the common misinterpretations of observations.
  • Counter misinformation with a multidisciplinary expert panel advising the lead organization.

Research

  • Facilitate open access and open data on UAPs, for transparency and to support research.
  • Conduct periodic surveys of Canadian perceptions, and measure whether the proposed service changes them.
  • Support citizen science and participatory science initiatives.
  • Provide tools for data collection — publicly accessible, portable, bilingual applications and interactive platforms.
  • Build on Canada's astronomy and aerospace research community, described in the report as "a superb talent pool and physical research infrastructure."

International collaboration

  • Information sharing through partnerships with AARO and NASA in the United States, GEIPAN in France, and SEFAA in Chile.
  • Cooperation in research and communication with those same partners.

Read it on science.gc.ca The 59-page PDF

Five gapsthe report names, in its own words: no cohesive and standardized reporting and follow-up system; no public engagement platform; reports not analysed unless they pose a safety or security risk; too little scientific involvement; and insufficient science literacy, which together "exacerbates the impact of misinformation and disinformation."

04

Requesting the records nobody has released

What the Sky Canada report did not resolve is still available under access to information. it is just slow, and the two best targets are known.

Target 1 — the Shag Harbour memo

The October 1967 memo that discounted aircraft, flares, floats and other known objects is signed by initials only, and the initials themselves are inconsistent between transcriptions: one reads Col. W. W. Turner, another reads Col. T. T. Turner. The file is identified as RG 24, accession 83-84-167, box 7523, file DRBS 3800-10-1 pt 1. That is exactly the kind of small, specific request that a diligent ATIP officer can actually fill.

RG 24acc. 83-84-167box 7523

Target 2 — Atlantic MERINT and provincial files

No list of Atlantic merchant-vessel MERINT incidents has been published. The Canadian Coast Guard ran the instruction; the reports went to its Western region. Separately, the Provincial Archives of New Brunswick hold the papers of Stanton Friedman, and whether those include sighting reports is an open question worth a letter.

Coast Guard MERINTPANB

How to actually file

Write to the institution that holds the file, name the record group and box numbers if you have them, describe the records and the date range, and ask for electronic copies. Access to information in Canada is about thirty days plus a possible extension, and fees apply only above a de minimis threshold. Be specific — a vague request about UFOs gets a vague refusal.

Access to information at DND

Target 3 — the modern reporting channel

There is no Canadian equivalent of the US all-domain anomaly office. Until the first Sky Canada recommendation is implemented, the only federal intake for a pilot or crew sighting is Transport Canada's CADORS occurrence report, and no analysis of what lands there has been published.

05

Everything here, machine-readable

If you are building something, start with these. They are small, stable, and documented enough to be worth citing.

  • data/releases.json
    40 release records: id, title, source agency, page count, date, type, and the direct file or source URL.
  • data/lac.json
    1,510 archival descriptions with record numbers, record groups, dates, locations and deep links into the live LAC record, assembled from 59 separate queries.
  • data/cases.json
    18 case files with coordinates, a sourcing grade, narrative detail, source links and linked media ids.
  • data/timeline.json
    80 years of the Canadian record, one entry per event.
  • data/media.json and data/podcasts.json
    Verified embed URLs, publishers and dates, with each item linked to the case it documents.
  • data/endpoints.json
    The verified endpoint list, as data rather than prose.
  • data/canada.json
    Province and territory boundaries from Natural Earth (public domain), simplified for the web. Use it to plot your own records.
  • llms.txt
    A plain-text summary of the archive, its key facts and every page, written for language models and answer engines.
